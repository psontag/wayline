Wayline · Issue #135 · Proposals for a decision

Tailwind trips

Five ways Wayline could plan around the wind. Each one shows what you would see, how it works and what it costs to build. They combine well, so the question is which to do first.

Recommendation
  1. Now: A (wind score on proposals) and B (ride it the other way, or start a day later). Small, uses the forecast we already load, no change to the route search.
  2. Next: D (less headwind for one day in the editor). It reuses the router with a wind cost.
  3. Later: C (a Tailwind proposal) once D's wind cost has proven itself, and E (typical wind) for trips more than 16 days out.

What we know about the wind

Forecast, up to 16 daysOpen-Meteo, hourly, per 0.1° cell. Already loaded for the weather view and the day timeline.
Head, cross, tail per dayAlready computed along each stage for the ride window. Proposals already show “Mostly tailwind” or “Headwind on day 2”.
Typical wind, any dateOpen-Meteo's historical archive gives wind per hour back to 1940. Averaging a month gives the usual direction and strength. Not loaded yet.
Forecasts shiftA 10-day forecast often turns 90°. Anything built on it must say how sure it is and update when the forecast changes.
A

Wind score on proposals

SmallForecast onlyRoutes unchanged

The generator works as today. Each proposal gets a headwind number for its dates: km ridden into the wind, weighted by its speed. The list can sort by it, and the best one gets a “Least headwind” tag. Each card shows a small bar per day.

  • Good when the proposals go in different directions.
  • Does nothing when all proposals share the same corridor.
  • Without a forecast the bar shows “no forecast yet”.
B

Ride it the other way, or start a day later

SmallForecast or typical windSame route

Often the cheapest fix is not a new route. A loop ridden the other way round, an A to B trip swapped to B to A, or a start one or two days later can turn most headwind into tailwind. Wayline checks these and shows a hint only when it helps a lot.

  • Loop direction and date shift work for any trip, also imported GPX.
  • Swapping start and end changes the train home. The hint says so.
  • One click applies it; undo is there as for every edit.
C

A Tailwind proposal

LargeForecast or typical windNew routes

The generator gets one more flavour next to Best, Flat and Hilly. Its route search makes ways into the wind more expensive, so it picks valleys and directions that run with the wind. It shows on the map in its own color, with its extra km against the best proposal.

cost = km × (1 + 0.6 × max(0, head km/h) / 25)
head = wind speed × cos(wind from − riding direction)
  • Each way's direction counts, so the search needs the riding direction and the hour of the day it is ridden.
  • Makes generation slower: wind per cell and hour must be ready before the search starts.
  • A one-way trip can't escape a strong headwind; the gain is mostly on loops.
D

Less headwind for one day

MediumForecastChanges one day

For a planned trip. When a day has a lot of headwind, its details show “Less headwind?”. Wayline routes that day again between the same stops with the wind cost from C, and shows the result dashed on the map with what changes. Apply or ignore.

  • Stops and hotels stay. Only the way between them changes.
  • Fits the forecast's 16 days: the trip is close, the forecast is good.
  • Can also suggest a later start time on that day, which the timeline already knows.
E

Plan with the typical wind

MediumHistorical dataFeeds A, B, C

Most trips are planned weeks ahead, where there is no forecast. For those, Wayline uses the usual wind for that month and region from past years. In Franconia that is mostly west to south-west in summer, so west-to-east trips and anticlockwise loops tend to win.

  • Shown as “usually” with a wind rose, never as a forecast.
  • Built once per region and month in the data pipeline, so the app makes no extra calls.
  • Switches to the forecast by itself when the trip is 16 days away.

Questions for you

1. How much extra riding is a tailwind worth?My default: up to 10 % more km, or 5 km per day, whichever is less. It sets the 0.6 in C and D.
2. May Wayline suggest swapping start and end?It changes the train home and often the stations. My default: yes for loops (direction only), ask for A to B trips.
3. Far-out trips: typical wind or nothing?My default: show typical wind, clearly marked “usually”, and switch to the forecast 16 days before.
4. Which first?My pick: A and B together in one PR, then D. Tell me if C (a Tailwind proposal) matters more to you.
