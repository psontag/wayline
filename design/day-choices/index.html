Stop and other ways in one place

Today a day has two sections: Stop (where the day ends) and Other ways (how you get there). Both answer the same question: how should this day go? Three ways to merge them, all shown on day 2 of Bamberg – Coburg.

Option 1

One list of choices

One section, "Where and which way". One table with every choice in the same columns. Towns (dashed pin) change where the day ends. Ways (color swatch) change the path to the same stop. "Find other ways" adds the ways to the list. Hover previews any row, click picks it.

Pros

  • One table, one way to compare: every row says what today and tomorrow become.
  • Sorted by today's km, so "a bit longer" choices of both kinds sit together.
  • Least new UI: the stop table grows a few rows.

Cons

  • Two kinds of row in one list. The icon has to carry the difference.
  • No way to combine both: another stop and another way.
Option 2

One section, two tabs

Same section, with a switch at the top: "End" lists other towns, "Way" lists other routes to the stop. Each tab keeps its own columns. The way tab searches when you open it.

Pros

  • Clear: one tab per question, each with the columns that fit it.
  • The slow search starts only when you want ways.

Cons

  • Still two lists, just stacked. You can't compare a town with a way at a glance.
  • Hidden: you have to know the Way tab is there.
Option 3

Stops with their ways

The stop table stays. The chosen stop gets a row of way chips under it: "Now", "via Schweinfurt", "Scenic". Pick a stop, then optionally a way to it. Picking another town and opening its chips searches ways to that town.

Pros

  • Matches the order you decide in: first where to sleep, then how to get there.
  • Can combine both: another town and another way to it.

Cons

  • Ways to another town need a new search each time, about 10 s.
  • Chips in a table row get tight on phones.
  • Most work of the three.
Recommendation

Option 1: one list of choices

  • It's what "combine them" means most directly: one place, one table, one hover-and-click behaviour.
  • Every row answers the same question with the same numbers, so towns and ways compare side by side.
  • It reuses what's built: the stop table, the routed ways, the hover preview. Option 3 can grow out of it later.
